\section{General information}
Walter is a small-form-factor IoT system-on-module (SoM) that combines a powerful ESP32-S3 system-on-chip (SoC) with a second-generation Sequans GM02SP LTE-M/NB-IoT 5G modem and GNSS receiver. Its ESP32-S3 provides many built-in peripherals as well, including UART, SPI, I²C, CAN, Wi-Fi b/g/n, and Bluetooth 5.

\section{Features}
The default version of Walter is based on an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R2 module (16MB flash, 2MB PSRAM, PCB antenna) with an on-board Sequans GM02SP modem. Versions with 8MB PSRAM and/or a u.FL connector for an external WiFi/BLE antenna are optional and available through direct ordering (see section \ref{orderable_parts}).
This combination makes Walter a unique development board that offers a rich feature-set which include but is not limited to:
\begin{itemize}
	\item CPU: Xtensa Dual-core 32-bit LX7 CPU (ESP32-S3 SoC)
	\item RAM: 2MB (Quad SPI) PSRAM, 8MB (Octal SPI) PSRAM optional
	\item Flash: 16MB (Quad SPI) Flash memory
	\item WiFi: 150Mbps(n) 802.11 WiFi b/g/n with on-board PCB antenna, u.FL connector optional
	\item LTE: CAT M1/NB1/NB2 (GM02SP module)
	\item GPS: GPS, GNSS Constellation support (GM02SP module)
	\item Bluetooth: 2Mbps Bluetooth 5 (LE), Bluetooth Mesh
	\item 24 physical GPIO pins
	\item Ultra low deep sleep current of 9.5\textmu A
	\item Open source software support for ESP-IDF, Arduino, Micropython and Toit
	\item Certified for CE, FCC, IC, UKCA, New-Zealand and Australia, and approved by Verizon
	\item Solderless integration into any project due to the 2.54mm pin headers
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Power} \label{power}
\subsubsection{Power Input} \label{power_input}
Walter can be powered either by connecting a USB-C cable or via the VIN pin (see pinout \ref{external_pins}). VIN accepts 3.0V to 5.5V. Everything on Walter works down to 3.0V, but radio performance is best between 3.3V and 5.5V, so we recommend that range for your design. Below 3.3V the DC-DC converter runs at 100\% duty cycle and the internal 3.3V rail follows VIN. The default brownout level of the ESP32-S3 is 2.44V.\\

The Sequans GM02SP modem is powered directly from VIN. Only the ESP32-S3 and the 3V3 OUT pin are powered through the DC-DC converter (see section \ref{power_output}). This is why the peak input current rises when VIN drops (see section \ref{power_consumption}).

\paragraph{Protection}
Walter has no overvoltage, surge or reverse polarity protection on VIN. The maximum VIN of 5.5V is also the absolute maximum rating of the modem supply, so VIN must never exceed 5.5V, including spikes caused by hot plugging, long supply cables or inductive loads. A standard TVS diode for 5V lines clamps well above 5.5V and is not sufficient on its own. When VIN comes from an unregulated or external source, place a regulator or an overvoltage protection switch in front of Walter. Add reverse polarity protection when the supply can be connected the wrong way round.

\paragraph{Bulk capacitance}
Place at least 2 $\times$ 10\textmu F 0603 ceramic capacitors (X5R or X7R) as close as possible to the VIN pin on the carrier board. They supply the current peaks during LTE transmission.

\paragraph{USB-C and VIN}
To minimize losses, the VBUS line of the USB-C connector and the VIN pin are directly connected to each other on Walter, without a diode. A voltage on one of them is therefore always present on the other:
\begin{itemize}
	\item When Walter is powered through USB-C, the 5V of the USB port is present on the VIN pin and feeds into the carrier board.
	\item When Walter is powered through VIN, the carrier supply is present on the USB-C connector and feeds into the USB port of the connected computer.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{DO NOT} connect a regular USB-C cable while the carrier board powers Walter through VIN, unless the carrier board is designed for it as described below. This can lead to seriously damaging the board, the computer and external peripherals connected to it! For occasional debugging, a USB-C cable with the power lead cut is the simplest solution.\\

To use a regular USB-C cable while Walter is powered from the carrier board, place an OR-ing element between the carrier supply and the VIN pin, as shown in Figure \ref{fig:vin_oring}. Whichever source has the highest voltage then powers Walter, and no current can flow from the USB port back into the carrier supply.
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Schottky diode:} the simplest solution, suited for a 5V carrier supply. Choose a part rated for at least 2A with a low forward voltage. VIN then sits a few hundred millivolts below the carrier supply, so a connected USB port takes over automatically.
	\item \textbf{Ideal diode:} an ideal diode IC, or an ideal diode controller with an external MOSFET. Use this when the carrier supply is low, for example a single Li-ion cell or a 3.3V to 4.2V rail, where the drop of a Schottky diode would push VIN below the recommended 3.3V. It must block reverse current and be rated for at least 1.5A peak. A single MOSFET used as reverse polarity protection is not sufficient, because it conducts in both directions once it is switched on.
\end{itemize}

The OR-ing element only protects the carrier supply. To also avoid feeding current into the computer, the voltage the carrier delivers on VIN must stay below the USB voltage (nominally 5V). A 5V supply with a Schottky diode, or a supply below 4.5V with an ideal diode, meets this. Do not connect other circuits of the carrier board to the VIN side of the OR-ing element, unless they may be powered from the USB port.

\subsubsection{Power Output} \label{power_output}
Walter contains a DC-DC converter which takes power from VIN and converts it to a regulated 3.3VDC supply. This rail powers the ESP32-S3 module and the 3V3 OUT pin. Below a VIN of 3.3V the converter runs at 100\% duty cycle and the 3.3V rail follows VIN.\\

The 3V3 OUT pin is switched by a P-channel MOSFET which is controlled by IO0 (pin 4):
\begin{itemize}
	\item The enable is active low: IO0 low switches 3V3 OUT on, IO0 high switches it off. IO0 is a strapping pin which is pulled high during reset, so 3V3 OUT is off while the ESP32-S3 boots. Pulling IO0 low externally during reset puts the ESP32-S3 in download (DFU) mode.
	\item The maximum load on 3V3 OUT is 250mA. The 3.3V rail is shared with the ESP32-S3, which needs the remaining capacity of the converter for WiFi and BLE transmission.
	\item The maximum capacitive load on 3V3 OUT is 1\textmu F. The switch has no slew rate control, so the load capacitance is charged from the 3.3V rail of the ESP32-S3 the moment 3V3 OUT is switched on. A larger capacitance causes a voltage dip that can reset the ESP32-S3.
	\item 3V3 OUT has no current limit or short-circuit protection. A short or an overload pulls down the supply of the ESP32-S3.
\end{itemize}

Loads that need more capacitance, 10\textmu F maximum, should use a load switch with slew rate control on the carrier board, or be powered from VIN. A dedicated point-of-load converter powered from VIN is typically an even better choice, and is required for loads above 250mA.

\subsection{WiFi/BLE antenna keepout} \label{antenna_keepout}
The PCB antenna of the ESP32-S3 module is located at the end of Walter opposite to the USB-C connector. Copper and metal close to the antenna detune it and reduce the WiFi and BLE range, so the carrier board must respect the following guidelines. They apply to the versions with a PCB antenna (P000000100 and P000000118). P000000119 has a u.FL connector for an external antenna and needs no keepout.
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Preferred:} place Walter at the edge of the carrier board, with the antenna end overhanging the edge or with the carrier board cut away below and next to the antenna.
	\item \textbf{Keepout zone:} keep an area of at least 24.8mm (the full width of Walter) by 6mm, measured from the antenna end of Walter, free of copper on all layers. No traces, ground pour, vias or components are allowed in this zone.
	\item \textbf{Alternative:} when the antenna cannot overhang, align the antenna end of Walter with the edge of the carrier board. Walter can then be placed anywhere along that edge, also in the middle. Extend the 6mm deep keepout zone along the board edge and make it as wide as possible on both sides of Walter.
	\item Keep metal parts such as enclosures, batteries, displays, screws and cables at least 15mm away from the antenna in all directions.
	\item Route USB, UART and switching power supply traces away from the antenna end.
\end{itemize}
Always verify the WiFi and BLE performance in the final product and enclosure.

\section{Software}
\subsection{Libraries and frameworks from QuickSpot}

Every Walter comes pre-flashed with an example application. This demonstration firmware requires the LTE and GNSS antennas to be connected and performs the following tasks: 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Configure the modem for LTE-M connectivity with the network-pushed APN.
    \item Check and update the GNSS assistance data if required.
    \item Try to establish a GNSS fix. The firmware attempts this up to 5 times, with a maximum duration of approximately 66 seconds per attempt.
    \item Upload the data to the \href{https://walterdemo.quickspot.io}{walterdemo.quickspot.io} portal.
\end{enumerate}

To use this pre-flashed demonstrator, install the LTE and GNSS antennas and insert a SIM card that supports LTE-M. Make sure the GNSS antenna has a clear view of the sky and allow Walter about 10 minutes to connect and upload its first message. Every Walter identifies itself using the MAC address printed on the bag in which it was shipped. \\

If you do not want to upload your position to the public \href{https://walterdemo.quickspot.io}{walterdemo.quickspot.io} platform, or if you want to run your own code, which is what Walter is designed for, you can flash your own firmware. You can easily program and upload your own firmware for Walter using Arduino, ESP-IDF, MicroPython or Zephyr. Please refer to the "Getting Started with Walter" guide available on our GitHub to get you up and running fast. The original demonstration firmware can be restored at any time with the online flasher (see section \ref{online_flasher}).

\subsection{Online flasher} \label{online_flasher}
Walter can be flashed straight from your browser with the online flasher at \url{https://www.quickspot.io/flash.html}, without installing drivers or a toolchain. Connect Walter to your computer with a USB-C cable, close any other program that uses its serial port and use a browser with Web Serial support, such as Chrome or Edge. The online flasher offers:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Ready-made firmware images, for example to update the firmware of the Sequans GM02SP modem or to restore the original factory firmware.
	\item Flashing your own firmware: upload a .bin file built with Arduino, ESP-IDF or PlatformIO. Use offset \verb+0x10000+ for application images and \verb+0x0+ for merged images. Optionally, the entire flash memory can be erased first.
	\item A serial terminal (9600 to 921600 baud) which opens automatically once flashing is done.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{AT commands}

The full set of supported AT commands can be found in the documentation of the Sequans GM02SP. This can be downloaded from this link: \href{https://www.renesas.com/eu/en/document/mah/ryz024-modules-command-users-manual?language=en}{https://www.renesas.com/eu/en/document/mah/ryz024-modules-command-users-manual?language=en}.

\subsection{Manual SFU of the GM02SP}

The easiest way to update the firmware of the GM02SP is the online flasher (see section \ref{online_flasher}). The manual procedure below is an alternative which uses the UART of the modem directly.\\

The second hardware UART of the GM02SP, namely \verb+UART1+, is used for manual firmware upgrades. To do a manual serial SFU of the GM02SP in Walter you must connect \verb+UART1+ via the testpads on the bottom of the module. Use an FTDI UART and make the following connections:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \verb+Walter RX1 <-> FTDI RX+
	\item \verb+Walter TX1 <-> FTDI TX+
	\item \verb+Walter RTS1 <-> FTDI RTS+
	\item \verb+Walter CTS1 <-> FTDI CTS+
	\item \verb+Walter GND <-> FTDI GND+
	\item \verb$Walter Vin <-> +5VDC$
\end{itemize}
Make sure that the 5V power source which is powering Walter and the FTDI share a common ground. To enable smooth updating it is best not to connect the USB-C on Walter to the computer on which the SFU software is running. Now follow the instructions which are bundled with the SFU release.
